### 2. Introduction (150–200 words)
- Hook: Parents invest in workbooks, tutoring, and apps — but many kids still freeze when a problem looks different
- The shift: Visual reasoning isn't about memorizing answers; it's about discovering rules through physical manipulation
- Promise of the post: what visual learning is, why it works, how Heir of Wisdom delivers it, and what realistic progress looks like
- CTA microcopy embedded: "Start Free Parent Onboarding"

### 3. What Is Visual Learning? (200–250 words)
- Definition: Learning through seeing, manipulating, and transforming visual information — not just reading text or memorizing answers
- Why it matters for kids 5–13: Spatial reasoning is a foundational cognitive skill that transfers to math, science, engineering, and everyday problem-solving
- Contrast with traditional drill apps: Rapid-fire multiple choice encourages quick guessing, not understanding

### 4. The Benefits of Visual Learning for Kids (300–400 words)
- **Benefit 1: Deep understanding, not surface memorization** — Kids isolate visual attributes (direction, color, count, size) instead of becoming overwhelmed by the whole graphic
- **Benefit 2: Transferable rules** — Once a child articulates a rule, they can apply it to completely different shapes and contexts (far-transfer)
- **Benefit 3: Confidence through hypothesis testing** — Children test ideas without penalty, building intuition rather than anxiety
- **Benefit 4: Parent visibility** — Parents see actual evidence (hint dependency, accuracy, transfer strength) rather than opaque scores

### 5. Why Heir of Wisdom Is Built for Visual Learning (300–400 words)
- Interactive canvas where children physically rotate, reflect, scale, and translate shapes
- Five-stage pedagogical engine: Observe → Experiment → Discover → Apply → Transfer
- Guided 3-tier scaffolding so children never feel helpless or judged
- Far-transfer challenges with completely different geometric objects to prove conceptual generalization
- Calm, beautiful architectural environment fostering quiet focus (no gamified distraction)
- Honest framing: Not "genius training," not IQ boosting — disciplined visual reasoning practice

### 6. Realistic Expectations (200–250 words)
- Improvement is stage-gated, not instant
- One incorrect answer is not a weakness; the system adapts after repeated difficulty
- No IQ-like scores, percentiles, or leaderboards
- Progress is visible on the parent dashboard (hint dependency, accuracy, transfer strength)
- Every child progresses at their own pace
