## Product Overview
**One-liner:** Heir of Wisdom is a premium visual reasoning and cognitive-learning platform that helps children discover spatial and logical rules through hands-on manipulation—not memorize drill answers.
**What it does:** An interactive visual reasoning studio for children ages 5–13. Every question comes to life through visualizations, explanations, and interaction. Children explore patterns, relationships, and the thinking behind an answer across CogAT, OLSAT, and NNAT-style reasoning domains (Verbal, Quantitative, Nonverbal; figure analogies, matrices, paper folding, classification, sequences, shape patterns, verbal analogies). Parents set goals; the platform designs the learning journey; the child experiences learning. A parent dashboard shows concise interpretation, evidence, and next steps—not IQ-like scores or percentiles.
**Product category:** Educational technology / cognitive development / gifted assessment preparation (entry points and milestones, not the entire product)
**Product type:** SaaS web application (Next.js 16 + React 19)
**Business model:** Freemium trial → paid subscription (parents sign up, try, pay)

## Target Audience
**Target market:** Primarily US/Western markets
**Decision-makers:** Parents of children aged 5–13
**Primary use case:** Help children develop independent, innovative thinking and transferable visual reasoning skills, with CogAT/OLSAT/NNAT preparation as entry points and milestones—not the entire product.
**Jobs to be done:**
- Let my child discover spatial and logical rules through hands-on manipulation, not memorization
- Help my child understand *why* an answer works, explore multiple approaches, and transfer reasoning to new forms
- See measurable cognitive development over time via a parent dashboard (interpretation + evidence + next step)
- Prepare for gifted assessment formats without test anxiety or negative marking
**Use cases:**
- Weekend cognitive enrichment at home
- Supplemental prep for gifted program screening (CogAT, OLSAT, NNAT)
- Screen-time alternative that builds quiet focus and transferable reasoning skills

## Problems & Pain Points
**Core problem:** Children can memorize drill answers but cannot transfer reasoning to unfamiliar formats; parents cannot see real cognitive growth.
**Why alternatives fall short:**
- Drill apps mark wrong answers red and create anxiety before high-stakes assessments
- Static worksheets offer zero way to experiment with rotation, flip, or scale
- Most brain-training apps gamify distraction rather than build quiet focus
- Conventional flow is QUESTION → ANSWER → NEXT (no exploration)
**What it costs them:** Money on ineffective enrichment, time shuttling to tutors, anxiety that "school smarts" won't equal real thinking
**Emotional tension:** Proud of the child's raw ability, anxious that school performance won't translate to independent problem-solving

## Competitive Landscape
**Direct:** Kumon, workbooks, CogAT/OLSAT/NNAT prep apps — fall short because they test memory of specific graphics instead of abstract rules.
**Secondary:** General brain-training / gamified learning apps — fall short because they optimize for engagement minutes, not transferable reasoning.
**Indirect:** Private tutors, doing nothing — fall short because they lack structured, stage-based progression and parent visibility.
**Affiliation rule:** Never imply official affiliation with CogAT, OLSAT, or NNAT publishers. State these are practice/coverage categories only.

## Differentiation
**Key differentiators:**
- Interactive canvas where kids physically rotate, reflect, scale, and translate shapes (Tactile Manipulation)
- Five-stage pedagogical engine: Observe → Experiment → Discover → Apply → Transfer
- Far-transfer challenges with completely different geometric objects to prove conceptual generalization
- Guided 3-tier scaffolding so children never feel helpless or judged
- Calm, beautiful architectural environment fostering quiet focus (no gamified distraction)
- Parent dashboard with granular data on hint dependency, practice accuracy, and transfer strength
- Mastery requires recognition, explanation/demonstration, application, and transfer to a materially different visual form
**How we do it differently:** The child experiences learning through a reasoning loop: Question → Think → Answer → Understand → Explore/manipulate → Transfer → Discovery/recognition.
**Why that's better:** Proves true conceptual mastery, not pattern memorization; builds confidence through hypothesis testing without negative marking.
**Why customers choose us:** They want their child to *see* what others miss, not just pick the right bubble.

## Objections
| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Will this help my child for real?" | Visual demo of physical manipulation + far-transfer challenges prove rule generalization, not memorization. Explanation sequences show the actual rule. |
| "How long before I see improvement?" | Parent dashboard shows hint dependency, accuracy, and transfer strength over time; stage-based progression gives visible milestones. No IQ-like scores or percentiles. |

**Anti-persona:** Parents seeking pure entertainment or gamified screen time; children under 5; families seeking guaranteed test-score improvements or "genius" outcomes.

## Switching Dynamics
**Push:** Frustration with drill apps that mark wrong answers red; worksheet fatigue; kid "aces school" but freezes on novel problems.
**Pull:** Physical shape manipulation, no negative marking, visible progress dashboard, architectural calm.
**Habit:** Existing subscription to Kumon, workbooks, or another brain-training app.
**Anxiety:** Will this actually transfer to school tests and gifted assessments?

**Words to avoid:** genius, IQ boost, drill, gamify, guaranteed, fast, easy, hack, proven outcomes (without evidence), research-backed (until substantiated), official affiliation (with test publishers)
**Glossary:**
| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| Far-transfer test | Applying a learned rule to completely different geometric objects to prove conceptual mastery |
| Tactile manipulation | Physically rotating, reflecting, scaling, and translating shapes on the interactive canvas |
| Rule articulation | The child stating the invariant rule governing a shape transition |
| Reasoning lenses | Rotation, Symmetry, Colour, Position — the child selects a lens and objects transform to reveal a relationship |
